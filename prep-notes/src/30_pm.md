# B3. Product Management

> Reading guide: Lead TPM / Associate Director interviews test judgement and leadership as much as craft. For each topic, have one real example with numbers, a framework you actually use, and a failure you learned from.

## B3.1 Strategy, vision and roadmap

> Interview angle: connect vision to outcomes, then to a sequenced roadmap with explicit trade-offs.

- **Product vision and strategy (including multi-year strategy)** — Vision: the future state you are building toward. Strategy: the choices (where to play, how to win), supported by diagnosis, guiding policy and coherent actions.
  - PM lens: a good strategy says what you will not do. Structure: customer problem → market and competitive context → differentiators → bets → success measures → risks.
  - Optum link: payment integrity and RCM strategies must reflect the shift from rules-only to real-time and AI-assisted, and from single-client builds to configurable platforms.
- **Product roadmap ownership and planning** — A communication and alignment tool showing intended outcomes over time.
  - PM lens: prefer outcome-based, time-horizon roadmaps (Now / Next / Later) with committed near-term scope and flexible later scope. Separate customer-facing roadmaps from delivery plans. Tie each theme to an objective and metric.
- **Portfolio strategy, portfolio backlog and portfolio prioritisation** — Allocating investment across multiple products or workstreams.
  - PM lens: use capacity allocation (for example: platform health, committed client work, compliance, innovation bets) and review quarterly. Show how you kill or merge low-value work.
- **Investment priorities and service model** — Deciding where funding goes and how a product is offered (tiers, SLAs, support).
  - PM lens: frame as an investment case with options, cost, benefit, risk and decision date.
- **Roadmap transparency for leadership and partners** — Predictable visibility and change management.
  - PM lens: publish a regular update with changes, reasons and impacts; maintain a decision log.
- **Translating strategic objectives into product requirements** — Cascade from objective to key result to epics.
  - PM lens: use OKRs or similar, and demonstrate traceability from business goal to story.
- **Aligning product investments to measurable business outcomes** — Outcome over output.
  - PM lens: define the leading indicator (adoption, usage) and lagging indicator (financial result) and the lag between them.
- **Culture of innovation; transformational opportunities** — Structured exploration beyond the core roadmap.
  - PM lens: use time-boxed experiments, clear stage gates and a pipeline of small bets alongside committed delivery.

## B3.2 Requirements and backlog

> Interview angle: show rigour in decomposition and clarity for engineers, especially for platforms with many teams.

- **PRDs / product requirement documentation** — Document capturing problem, users, goals, scope, requirements, success metrics, dependencies, risks, rollout.
  - PM lens: keep it living and concise; include explicit non-goals and open questions. For regulated work add compliance and audit requirements.
- **Epics, features, user stories, enablers** — Hierarchy from large outcome to small deliverable. Enablers are technical or architectural work that unlock future value (for example data model changes, infrastructure).
  - PM lens: make enablers visible in the backlog with the value they unlock; otherwise they lose to features.
- **User stories and the INVEST criteria** — Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable.
  - PM lens: write stories for a user role and outcome, not for a component; slice vertically so each story delivers value end to end.
- **Acceptance criteria** — Testable conditions for "done", often in Given/When/Then form.
  - PM lens: include edge cases, error handling, performance, security and audit logging where relevant; get engineers and QA to review.
- **Business rules and process flows** — Explicit rules (thresholds, eligibility logic) and flow diagrams.
  - PM lens: store rules in a version-controlled, testable form; payment integrity depends on this.
- **Functional and non-functional requirements (NFRs)** — What the system does vs how well (performance, availability, security, scalability, observability, compliance).
  - PM lens: quantify NFRs (for example p95 latency under a number of milliseconds, availability target, recovery objectives).
- **Technical specifications** — Detailed design documents typically owned by engineering and architecture, with PM input on requirements.
  - PM lens: review for requirement coverage and trade-offs, not for code details.
- **Backlog creation, refinement/grooming and maintenance** — Continuous process to keep the backlog ordered and ready.
  - PM lens: maintain at least two sprints of ready work; hold regular refinement sessions with engineers; prune stale items.
- **Backlog readiness (JD6)** — A story is ready when it is understood, estimated, sequenced, dependencies known and acceptance criteria clear before planning.
  - PM lens: track a "ready ratio" and rework caused by unclear requirements.
- **Decomposing complex capabilities** — Breaking a large capability into thin slices that can ship independently.
  - PM lens: techniques include workflow steps, rules variations, data segments, user roles, and happy path first.
- **Translating business requirements into technical solutions** — Bridge between operational need and architecture.
  - PM lens: ask "what decision or action does this enable?" and agree on the simplest design that meets NFRs.
- **Requirements gathering; closing requirements gaps** — Interviews, observation, process mapping, data review.
  - PM lens: confirm understanding with a sample of real cases and prototype early.
- **Traceability** — Linking initiative → feature → story → test → release.
  - PM lens: especially important for audit and compliance; use tool integrations (Aha! with Rally/Jira).
- **Definition of Done (DoD)** — Team agreement on completion, beyond functional behaviour: tests passed, security checks, documentation, monitoring, release readiness.
  - PM lens: JD4 and JD9 both emphasise a rigorous DoD; mention including operational readiness.

## B3.3 Prioritisation and decision-making

> Interview angle: show a framework, but also show judgement about when frameworks mislead.

- **Prioritisation frameworks** — Common ones: RICE (reach, impact, confidence, effort), WSJF (weighted shortest job first, used in SAFe), MoSCoW, Kano, value vs effort, cost of delay.
  - PM lens: choose by context: WSJF for portfolio sequencing across teams, RICE for feature comparison, cost of delay for compliance deadlines. Keep scoring transparent and revisit.
- **Sequencing by business value, operational impact and delivery readiness** — Practical ordering lens (JD9).
  - PM lens: add dependency and risk to the mix; explain how you handle hard deadlines (regulatory) versus optional value.
- **Cost/benefit analysis and forecast tracking** — Compare expected benefits to costs; track actual versus forecast.
  - PM lens: use NPV/ROI/payback; include run costs (cloud, support, model usage). Revisit forecasts after launch and learn.
- **Business case development** — Problem, options, benefits, costs, risks, timeline, decision.
  - PM lens: build with finance partners; show sensitivity analysis and what you need to believe.
- **Win/loss analysis (JD5)** — Review deals or decisions won and lost to learn why.
  - PM lens: systematically interview sales, customers and prospects; feed insights to the roadmap.
- **Trade-offs across customer, engineering, security and business priorities (JD7)** — Core of senior product leadership.
  - PM lens: use a decision document (options, criteria, recommendation, owner) and make the trade-off visible rather than hidden.
- **Decisions under ambiguity and incomplete information** — Reversible vs irreversible decisions; speed vs certainty.
  - PM lens: decide fast on reversible choices, run experiments for uncertain ones, escalate irreversible ones with a clear recommendation.

## B3.4 Discovery, customer insight and market intelligence

> Interview angle: show you combine qualitative and quantitative input, and that internal users count as customers.

- **Voice of the Customer (VoC) reporting and analysis** — Collecting and synthesising feedback from surveys, support, reviews and interviews.
  - PM lens: build a repeatable cadence; tag themes; link to metrics and releases; close the loop with customers.
- **Customer feedback analysis (qualitative and quantitative)** — Mix of verbatims and metrics (NPS, CSAT, task success).
  - PM lens: triangulate. A theme is stronger when it appears in reviews, support tickets and usage data.
- **App-store ratings, reviews, sentiment and feature requests** — Public feedback on mobile apps.
  - PM lens: monitor rating trends by version, response rates and recurring defects.
- **Competitor monitoring** — Track competitor reviews, features and keywords.
  - PM lens: look for gaps and table-stakes features; do not copy blindly.
- **App-store optimisation (ASO)** — Improving discoverability and conversion (keywords, screenshots, ratings).
  - PM lens: relevant mainly to consumer-facing apps (JD1).
- **Market research and industry trends** — Analyst reports, regulatory change, competitor moves.
  - PM lens: summarise "so what" for the roadmap.
- **User research, discovery and feedback sessions** — Interviews, observation, usability tests, prototypes.
  - PM lens: continuous discovery habits (weekly customer touchpoints), jobs-to-be-done framing, and testing with real work samples (for example actual claims in a sandbox).
- **Customer experience measurement; integrated view of CX** — Combine feedback sources.
  - PM lens: a unified dashboard and a monthly insights narrative.
- **Early-signal development; routing signals to owners (JD1)** — Detect emerging defects or friction early and route to the right team.
  - PM lens: define thresholds, owners and response time.
- **Benchmarking and evaluation of business processes; process improvement** — Understand current-state workflow and measure improvement.
  - PM lens: time-and-motion samples, value stream mapping and baselines before launch.

## B3.5 Product lifecycle and launch

> Interview angle: demonstrate end-to-end ownership including adoption and retirement.

- **End-to-end product lifecycle** — Ideation → discovery → build → launch → grow → optimise → sunset.
  - PM lens: explicitly plan retirement of low-value features, and migrations.
- **Product launches and adoption** — Launch plan: audience, positioning, enablement, support, rollout, metrics.
  - PM lens: for enterprise products, adoption is the hard part (training, change management, integration). Plan champion users and early-adopter feedback.
- **Continuously improving features; retiring underperforming ones** — Regular usage and value reviews.
  - PM lens: set criteria (usage below a threshold, cost to maintain) and communicate sunset timelines.
- **Alpha, Beta, Production and commercialisation readiness (JD3)** — Staged release: internal testing, limited external pilots, general availability. Readiness includes support model, pricing or chargeback, security reviews, documentation, legal and compliance sign-off.
  - PM lens: define exit criteria for each stage.
- **Multi-year customer deployment plans; rollout strategy (JD4)** — Phased onboarding of customers or business units.
  - PM lens: wave planning, shared playbooks, risk-based sequencing, and a feedback loop to the roadmap.
- **System demos to stakeholders and customers** — Regular demonstration of working software.
  - PM lens: demo outcomes and real data; capture decisions and actions.
- **Operational readiness** — Runbooks, monitoring, incident process, training.
  - PM lens: add to DoD and release checklists.
- **Release plans and release orchestration** — Coordinated release across teams.
  - PM lens: feature flags and phased rollouts reduce risk.

## B3.6 Metrics, KPIs and analytics

> Interview angle: have a metrics tree for a product and explain leading versus lagging indicators.

- **KPIs, success metrics and product health indicators** — Measures of outcomes and health.
  - PM lens: build a metrics tree from north star to input metrics. Examples for payment integrity: audit yield, savings realised, overturn rate, provider abrasion, cycle time, cost per reviewed claim, automation rate.
- **Product adoption and feature utilisation** — Activation, active use, depth, retention.
  - PM lens: instrument events early; separate "available" from "used".
- **Delivery predictability and platform scalability** — Engineering outcomes: commitment reliability, throughput, lead time; scaling limits.
  - PM lens: use DORA metrics (deployment frequency, lead time, change failure rate, time to restore).
- **Operational efficiency, business impact and financial impact** — Cost, time and revenue effects.
  - PM lens: agree definitions with finance in advance.
- **Audit yield, throughput and cycle-time reduction (JD9)** — Domain-specific measures.
  - PM lens: show baseline, target and actual in stories.
- **Customer satisfaction and onboarding speed (JD7)** — Platform success measures.
  - PM lens: developer NPS, time to first successful call, support ticket volume.
- **Data-driven / data-informed decision-making** — Use evidence while retaining judgement.
  - PM lens: describe a decision where data contradicted intuition.
- **Dashboards, scorecards and recurring reporting** — Regular visibility.
  - PM lens: keep to a small set of decision-useful metrics; define owners and cadence.
- **Business MBOs and value delivery (JD5)** — Management-by-objectives targets.
  - PM lens: align product goals with MBOs and report progress transparently.

## B3.7 Platform and technical product management

> Interview angle: this is the heart of a Lead TPM role. Be able to explain how a platform earns adoption.

- **Platform products supporting multiple downstream applications** — Shared capabilities consumed by many teams or clients.
  - PM lens: treat internal consumers as customers; maintain a consumer council; publish roadmap and SLAs.
- **Reusable services; speed to market and reuse** — Shared components reduce duplicated work.
  - PM lens: measure reuse rate and time to onboard a new consumer; avoid a "platform nobody wants" by validating demand first.
- **Configurability, extensibility and governance** — Tenant-specific settings, plug-ins, controls.
  - PM lens: draw a clear line between configuration (no code), extension (supported hooks) and customisation (discouraged).
- **Product boundaries, ownership and integration points (JD7)** — Clear statements of what the platform owns and what it does not.
  - PM lens: publish a responsibility matrix (RACI) and API contracts.
- **Service tiers, SLOs/SLAs and consumption models** — Differentiated service levels and pricing or chargeback.
  - PM lens: SLI (what you measure), SLO (target), SLA (commitment); use error budgets to balance reliability and feature work.
- **Developer experience; self-service onboarding; platform APIs** — Documentation, SDKs, sandboxes, templates, automation.
  - PM lens: reduce time to first success; measure it.
- **Productising infrastructure as consumable services** — Packaging complex capabilities into simple offerings.
  - PM lens: write a service catalogue entry: purpose, who uses it, how to start, limits, price, support, SLO.
- **Platform economics** — Cost to build and run vs value delivered; chargeback or showback.
  - PM lens: include unit costs (per request, per GPU hour) in reviews.
- **API product management** — Treat APIs as products: versioning, deprecation policy, backwards compatibility, developer portal.
  - PM lens: communicate breaking changes early and run version support windows.

## B3.8 Product operations and governance

> Interview angle: show scalable operating rhythm, especially across geographies.

- **Product intake management** — Standard way requests enter and are triaged.
  - PM lens: a single intake form with required context, scoring and SLA for response.
- **Aha hygiene; decision logs; action-item tracking** — Keeping tools accurate and decisions recorded.
  - PM lens: weekly hygiene checks; decisions logged with owner and rationale.
- **Product documentation** — Living documents, knowledge base.
  - PM lens: single source of truth; version and review.
- **Release governance and readiness** — Checklists, readiness signals, risk escalation.
  - PM lens: go/no-go criteria with named approvers.
- **Standard templates and repeatable workflows** — Consistency across teams and locations.
  - PM lens: templates for PRDs, business cases, launch plans, QBRs.
- **Monthly operating review; executive narrative; KPI story** — Regular leadership forum.
  - PM lens: lead with outcomes, risks and decisions needed, not activity.
- **Communication calendar and recurring stakeholder updates** — Planned cadence.
  - PM lens: tailor content by audience; keep short.

## B3.9 Product tools

- **Aha!** — Product roadmap and strategy tool (goals, initiatives, features, ideas portal, release planning). Named in JD1, JD2, JD5, JD6.
  - PM lens: use for strategy-to-feature traceability and roadmap views.
- **Rally** — Agile planning and tracking (stories, iterations, releases, portfolio). Named in JD3, JD5, JD6.
  - PM lens: sync feature-level data from Aha!; track capacity and dependencies.
- **Jira** — Issue and backlog tracking widely used by engineering teams.
  - PM lens: standard workflows, epics-stories, dashboards and filters.
- **Azure DevOps** — Microsoft's suite for boards, repos, pipelines and test plans.
  - PM lens: boards plus pipelines integration for traceability from story to release.
- **Tool-integration principle** — Strategy lives in the roadmap tool, delivery in the Agile tool, with automated sync and unique IDs.
  - PM lens: this answers "how do you keep traceability across initiatives, features and stories?"
