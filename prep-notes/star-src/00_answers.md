# 1. Core Interview Answers (Spoken Versions)

> Read these out loud, then put them in your own words. Each one runs about 2 to 2.5 minutes at a normal speaking pace (300 to 350 words). If you want 3 minutes, add one more example from the stories below. Every example comes from your resume or case-study notes and carries a number, an award or a client sign-off. Client names stay out because of your NDA.

Today I'm a Senior Product Manager at o9 Solutions, where I own the Linear Programming Solver, a product above $5 million in ARR, along with the Supply Chain Solver, platform engineering and our enterprise AI agents. Some of the results I'm proud of: we cut optimization runtime by 65 percent, brought customer onboarding time down by 75 percent, and moved a monolithic platform to cloud-native services that now supports over 1,000 concurrent users, up from about 40, with roughly $900K a year in infrastructure savings. I lead a Senior PM and two PMs, I'm working at Senior PM II or Associate Director scope, and I'm in this year's promotion cycle. I also received the Business Milestone Award in two quarters of 2026. Across that client work I delivered a GenAI assistant for a scientific-instruments client that cut answer time on complex product questions from hours to seconds, with retrieval precision above 95 percent, and a GenAI content platform for a global pharma company that made content creation about 75 percent faster. On a digital healthcare platform, patient satisfaction went up 30 percent. If I step back, the thread is enterprise platforms, AI that has to be accurate and explainable, and outcomes you can measure. Healthcare is where I want to apply that at a much bigger scale, and that's what brought me to this role at Optum.

- Spoken length: about 2 to 2.5 minutes.

## 1.3 Why switch from a supply chain SaaS company to healthcare, and how would you pitch yourself as a healthcare platform and AI product manager?

I see it less as switching industries and more as taking the same craft somewhere it matters more. When planners couldn't tell why a model was infeasible, we built a diagnosis capability that cut debugging effort by 60 percent, and an AI explanation layer that cut interpretation effort by about half in pilots. That's the same problem healthcare has with claims, prior authorization and audits: a system makes a call, and a person has to trust it and sometimes challenge it. I also haven't only worked in supply chain. In healthcare I've worked on a digital healthcare platform where patient satisfaction rose 30 percent, a digital pharmacy experience for a US healthcare services provider where our API-first redesign supported about 7.6 percent growth in online sales in the comparable November period, and GenAI-assisted data engineering for a large health insurer that cut estimated delivery effort by more than 1,000 hours across over 300 data sources. So here's how I'd pitch myself. The first part is platforms that scale: I led the move from a monolith to cloud-native services, took concurrent users from about 40 to over 1,000, and cut downtime by 30 percent. The second is AI people trust: our scientific assistant went from hours to seconds with over 95 percent retrieval precision and formal sign-off from the client's scientists, and the pharma content platform cut content creation time by about 75 percent with compliance reviewers built into the workflow. The third is learning a new domain quickly and getting results in it. At Publicis Sapient I worked across seven industries, and this year I received the Business Milestone Award in two quarters at o9. For this role I'm already going deep on claims and payment integrity, including how pre-pay and post-pay review work and what audit yield really measures, so I can be useful in the first few weeks.
