# 2. How to Use This Story Bank

> What this is: fifteen STAR stories built only from your resume (Parts 1 to 4) and the healthcare case-study bullets you pasted, plus ten skeletons for leadership and behavioral questions that need your own facts. Clients are described generically because of your NDA.

## 1.1 Rules that keep these answers credible

- **Every number is labelled.** Achieved = stated as a result in your material. Targeted = a goal. Estimated or projected = a model, not an outcome. Say the label out loud ("we targeted", "the business case estimated").
- **Nothing is invented.** Situations and tasks are restated from your bullets. Where a good interviewer will ask "and what did you personally do or decide?", the story has a **Strengthen** line listing what to add from memory (the pushback, the trade-off, who disagreed). Add those before the interview; they make the story yours.
- **Client names stay out.** Use the generic descriptor given in each story. If asked: "That engagement is under NDA. I can go as deep as you like on the problem, the decisions and the results."
- **Length:** 90 to 120 seconds spoken. Lead with the result in one sentence, then Situation, Task, Action, Result. Cut to the two most relevant actions when time is short.
- **Confirm before use:** your exact role, team size, duration and timeframe for each client engagement. Your resume does not state them for the case studies.

# 3. STAR Stories, Part 1: Platforms and Explainability

## Story 1. Solver modernisation and scaling a $5M+ ARR platform

- Use for: roadmap trade-offs, platform ownership, "how do you balance modernisation against customer requests", scaling and cost.
- Situation: I own a linear programming solver that powers supply, production, deployment and network planning for enterprise customers. It had grown into a $5M+ ARR product, and customers needed it to handle larger models and express their real business policies.
- Task: set the roadmap and investment priorities, balancing customer requests, platform modernisation and long-term technology bets, so performance, adoption and commercial value all improved.
- Action:
  - I prioritised solver modernisation so planners could evaluate significantly larger optimisation models with faster response.
  - I invested in solver architecture changes to improve scalability.
  - I introduced advanced proportional sourcing constraints and configurable optimisation logic so models matched real business policies.
  - I added tenant-level resource management to control infrastructure cost, and telemetry-driven monitoring for performance and convergence visibility.
- Result: optimisation runtime down 65% (achieved); scalability up 20 to 30% (achieved); sourcing optimisation accuracy up 40% (achieved); infrastructure cost down 35% (achieved); product positioned at $5M+ ARR.
- Strengthen: what you chose not to build to make room for modernisation; who disagreed and how you resolved it; the period over which ARR reached $5M+ and your share of that growth versus the team's.
- Caution: be precise about "scaled into $5M+ ARR". Describe your role (product owner and strategy lead) and what you drove personally.

## Story 2. Making solver decisions explainable and debuggable

- Use for: explainability, human-in-the-loop design, decision transparency. This is your strongest analogy for explaining why a claim or audit finding was flagged.
- Situation: planners found it hard to interpret optimisation outputs and to understand why a model was infeasible. Debugging conflicting constraints took too long, which reduced trust.
- Task: make solver decisions understandable and debuggable so planners could act with confidence.
- Action:
  - I designed an infeasibility diagnosis capability that surfaces conflicting constraints and bottlenecks.
  - I developed AI-assisted insights that translate mathematical outputs into business recommendations in natural language.
  - I partnered with data science on pilot evaluations to test whether the explanations reduced effort.
- Result: solver debugging effort down 60% (achieved); interpretation effort down about 50% in pilot evaluations (pilot result, not production).
- Strengthen: how you chose the format of explanations; one planner reaction; how you checked that explanations were correct.
- Caution: your resume describes the explainability agent as "conceptualised" in one place and as developed with pilot results in another. Decide exactly what shipped, what was piloted and what was a concept, and say so.

- Action:

## Story 4. Moving a monolith to cloud-native services with observability

- Use for: architecture trade-offs, technical debt, cloud, DevOps, reliability, platform product management.
- Situation: our enterprise platform APIs and developer infrastructure were monolithic. Scalability was limited to about 40 concurrent users, production issues were frequent, and troubleshooting was slow for 200+ enterprise customers.
- Task: lead the modernisation to cloud-native services with built-in observability so the platform could support enterprise-grade deployments.
- Action:
  - I directed modernisation of the APIs and developer infrastructure to cloud-native services.
  - I led an AI-native observability strategy with centralised monitoring and telemetry pipelines, including continuous centralised monitoring that caught execution failures early.
  - I migrated 13 integration workflows to a next-generation framework and automated release validation, regression testing and deployment through CI/CD.
  - I used voice-of-customer input to prioritise platform investments.
- Result: concurrent users from 40 to 1,000+; production downtime down 30%; about $900K annual infrastructure savings; API-related production issues down 90%; issue diagnosis effort down 40%; release preparation from multiple days to a few hours (all achieved as stated).
- Strengthen: the hardest trade-off (feature roadmap versus modernisation) and how you sold the investment; sequencing and rollback plan; how you worked with architects.
- Caution: explain your product role versus engineering's technical choices.
